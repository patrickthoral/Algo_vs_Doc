# Statistical analysis

## Outcome and analysis populations

The primary outcome is ICU readmission or death within 7 days after ICU discharge and before
discharge from the index hospital. HDU readmission is reported separately for VUmc and is not part
of the common primary outcome because it cannot be ascertained at LUMC. Transfers to another
hospital are excluded, and events after discharge from the index hospital are not observable.
Palliative-care and do-not-readmit stays are excluded by the original enrolment criteria.

The paired performance population requires both a physician prediction and the model prediction at
the physician-assessment time. Prediction availability is described in the full outcome-eligible
population. Model performance at ICU discharge is also evaluated in that full population as a
selection sensitivity analysis.

## Primary and secondary performance comparisons

The co-primary AUROC contrasts compare the model prediction at the physician-assessment time with the
physician percentage estimate and with the physician ordinal category. Raw and Holm-adjusted
two-sided p-values are reported across this two-comparison family.

The prespecified added-value AUROC family contains:

- percentage-based physician-model combination versus physician percentage;
- percentage-based physician-model combination versus model;
- category-based physician-model combination versus physician category; and
- category-based physician-model combination versus model.

Raw and Holm-adjusted two-sided p-values are reported for this four-comparison family. The direct
paired contrast between the physician ordinal category and percentage estimate is a supportive
elicitation-format analysis and is reported without a multiplicity-adjusted p-value.

AUROC is the primary discrimination summary. AUROC confidence intervals and differences use
patient-cluster bootstrap samples so that all stays from a sampled patient are resampled together.
Area under the precision-recall curve (AUPRC) is calculated as average precision and reported as
a supplementary discrimination summary because the outcome is uncommon. The two
model-versus-physician AUPRC contrasts form a secondary family with Holm-adjusted p-values. Other
new AUROC and AUPRC contrasts are exploratory and use unadjusted p-values. These comparisons do
not replace the prespecified primary AUROC comparison. The final analysis requests 10,000
replicates and reports the number of valid replicates.

All stochastic analyses use the fixed seed supplied by the analysis notebook (`random_seed`,
default 42). Bootstrap replicate statistics are persisted as CSV files in `results/bootstrap/`.
A separate CSV records the requested replicate count and seed.

## Combined predictions

Physician percentage and model probabilities are clipped to `[0.000001, 0.999999]`, transformed to
logits, and combined by logistic regression. In the category-based combination, medium and high
physician categories are represented by separate indicators with low as the reference. Predictions
are generated by stratified, patient-grouped cross-validation. Combined-model inference uses a
nested patient-cluster bootstrap that repeats the complete grouped cross-validation fit within each
replicate. This propagates variation from both patient sampling and estimation of the combination
model.

## Calibration and operating characteristics

Discrimination reporting includes AUROC and AUPRC. AUPRC is calculated as average precision by
weighting precision by the increase in recall at each observed prediction threshold. Calibration
reporting includes the calibration intercept, calibration slope, observed-to-expected ratio, Brier
score, and calibration plots. Confidence intervals use patient-clustered bootstrap percentiles.

Fixed selection budgets identify the highest-risk 5%, 10%, and 15% of discharges. They report the
number selected, events captured, sensitivity, specificity, positive and negative predictive values, and F1,
with patient-clustered percentile intervals. Stable row order resolves ties so that the requested
number is selected. These are operational ranking analyses, not reusable probability thresholds.

For the conceptual implementation figure, fixed predicted-risk thresholds of 5%, 10%, and 20% are
also summarized with the resulting number and proportion selected and events captured. These
thresholds are illustrative operating points rather than prespecified or validated clinical decision
thresholds.

The empirical threshold maximizing the Youden index is retained only as an exploratory
supplementary analysis. Its operating characteristics are apparent estimates because threshold
selection and evaluation use the same cohort. It is not proposed as a clinical decision threshold.

Only physicians supplied prespecified low, medium, and high categories. Model and combined
predictions are not retrospectively divided into categories using outcome-informed cut points.
For discrimination analyses, the physician categories are encoded as the ordered score 0, 1, and
2. AUROC depends only on this ordering, not on the numerical spacing. This score is not interpreted
as a probability, so calibration and Brier-score statistics are not reported for it. In the
category-based combined model, medium and high are entered as separate indicators with low as the
reference category. The combination therefore does not impose equal spacing or a linear category
effect.

## Prediction availability

Physician-prediction availability is described separately for VUmc and LUMC in the
outcome-eligible population. The supplementary table compares characteristics of stays with and
without a physician prediction and reports the availability denominators without row-level
hypothesis tests.

Notebook 2 also fits a separate exploratory logistic regression model for each hospital. Each
model adjusts for age, SOFA at discharge, categorical admission type, ICU census at discharge,
same-day external transfers, same-day elective-surgery ICU admissions, discharge after 18:00, and
weekend discharge. Admission type uses `other_or_unknown` as its reference category. Standard
errors use two-way clustering by patient and local discharge date with the available finite-sample
correction. Adjusted odds ratios, 95% confidence intervals, and cluster counts are saved to a local
CSV and displayed in notebook 2. They are not included in the generated supplementary Word table.

Inverse-probability weighting is not used. It cannot reconstruct missing physician predictions,
would require an untestable missing-at-random assumption, and would be unstable with the observed
number of outcomes. The descriptive availability comparison, exploratory models, and full-eligible
model-at-discharge analysis describe the observed selection process without claiming to remove
selection bias.

## Descriptive and subgroup reporting

The main report contains center-specific cohort characteristics, the enrollment flow, primary event
components, distinct event-patient counts, and core event-versus-no-event characteristics.

Detailed tables by specialty, admission type, discharge care level, ICU length-of-stay group,
confidence level, readmission pathway, and low-risk event definition are supplementary and
descriptive. They are not used for formal interaction or subgroup-superiority claims. Granular
low-risk characteristics are suppressed when fewer than 10 events satisfy a definition, while
aggregate ICU-readmission, death, and overlap counts remain reportable.
